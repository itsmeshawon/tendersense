# ADR 0006 — Scope adjustments per SoT revision 2026-09-09

**Status:** Accepted · **Date:** 2026-09-11 · **Phase:** 1 (planning)

## Context

A strategy revision to the Source of Truth arrived as `raw/TenderSense-SOT-Changes (1).md` (dated 2026-09-09, for team meeting). It doesn't overturn the build plan; it reshapes scope, ordering, and vocabulary — most of it clarifying what makes the product different from competitors, not what the tools are. Because Phase 0 shipped days earlier and Phase 1 hasn't started execution, the cost of folding these in now is at its lowest.

## Decisions

### 1. Adapter ordering — e-GP first, BPPA later

Original plan: BPPA advertisement pages first, e-GP later.

Revised: **World Bank + e-GP notices in Phase 1; BPPA / NOA / APP in Phase 2.**

Reason: the e-GP tender notice page carries the eligibility text (turnover, past contract size, cash-in-hand, years of experience). Without it, Phase 4 assessment has nothing to assess. BPPA advertisements are a discovery-only feed that duplicates less useful data.

### 2. "Awards" is three distinct e-GP surfaces, not one

Split explicitly:

- **eExperience** (a company's own past contracts, with certificate numbers) — Phase 1, **on-demand lookup only** (no cron, no source_records, no revision tracking). Feeds profile-onboarding "type your company name and see your history appear."
- **eContracts / NOA** (who won what, for how much) — Phase 2. Feeds the "who has won similar work" affordance from SoT-Changes #8.
- **APP** (published Annual Procurement Plans) — Phase 2. Forward-looking differentiator ("this tender is coming in ~4 months, here is the certificate you'll need").

Only eExperience is on the Phase 1 critical path; the other two are additive.

### 3. Retain notice payloads in `source_records.payload`

Original SoT §9.2 said "do not store everything." Revised: distinguish notice pages (KB) from attached tender-document PDFs (MB). **Store notice HTML/JSON.** Do not store the attached tender documents.

Reason: parser bugs will surface for months; without retained payloads, every fix requires re-crawling the source, which is slow and rude.

### 4. Eligibility is a checklist with hard-fail, not a percentage

SoT §22 gives a status enum (meets/partial/needs_verification/gap/n.a.); SoT §23 immediately reduces it to "Eligibility: 78%". These two views of the same thing disagree. A number hides disqualification.

**Kill `eligibility_score` from `public.assessments` when Phase 4 lands.** Show a checklist. Mark hard-fail items prominently ("you cannot bid on this, and here is why"). Only score items where the company can actually bid.

Recorded here so the Phase 4 plan starts from this position, not the SoT default.

### 5. Grade + decision — no other scales on screen

Four parallel scales (match score, weighted category scores, five-level recommendation, three-way decision) reduce to two visible scales:

- **Grade** — an A/B/C/D letter paired with a word (**A — Strong fit / B — Good fit / C — Possible / D — Weak fit**). Not "S tier" — gaming vocabulary reads oddly to BD product managers and breaks the "each letter says something plain" property.
- **Decision** — pursue / hold / decline (unchanged from SoT §24).

Two non-grades that must not look like a D:

- **Not eligible** — hard-fail on a make-or-break requirement. Excluded with a reason, not ranked at the bottom.
- **Need more info** — profile is missing something; carries a button to fill the gap.

Numeric match score kept in the DB for sorting; not shown to users. Recommendation-level (Strong Pursue / etc.) collapsed into the grade + reason line.

Recorded here; UI-side implementation lands with Phase 3 (matching).

### 6. Grade boundaries — hardcoded in one file, revisited on data

For MVP: grade thresholds live in a single named constant in one file, not scattered through the matching code. No user-configurable tuning knobs.

But: calibrated against ~40 labelled tenders in Phase 3 week 1; re-checked after the first month of pilot data. "Hardcoded" means "not user-configurable," not "frozen."

### 7. MVP scope reductions

**Individual profiles hidden from UI, kept in schema.**

- `/workspaces/new` no longer shows the `individual` radio option.
- `public.workspaces.workspace_type` column stays; enum values `individual` and `organization` unchanged.
- Existing test workspaces of type `individual` remain untouched.
- Turning it back on later is a UI change, not a migration.

Rationale: individuals need a genuinely different set of fields and a donor-weighted feed. Half-building both audiences produces a form people abandon. Ship organization well first.

**Team features, reports, exports pushed post-MVP.**

- Phase 5 (Collaboration) mostly gutted. Retained for MVP: single-user Pro plan. Dropped: comments, task assignment, opportunity ownership, decision history for teams.
- Phase 6 (Alerts / Reporting) trimmed. Retained: revision detection + amendment alerts to a single Pro user, daily digest to that user. Dropped: team reports, pipeline reports, exports.

Reason: BRAC IT needs to first tell us matching is good enough to be worth organising a team around. Otherwise Phases 5–6 slip and take the rest with them.

### 8. PII stance on named procuring-entity officials

Notice pages carry named officials with direct phone numbers. This is personal data about identifiable people, even though the source is public.

Phase 1 rule (locked here so the e-GP adapter starts from it):

- **Store** the field in `source_records.payload` (raw HTML/JSON) so re-parsing after selector drift stays cheap.
- **Do not index** in the `opportunities` table as structured columns.
- **Do not display** on list views (`/opportunities` list shows organization / ministry only).
- **Do display** on the detail page for authenticated workspace members. No public exposure.

When document upload arrives on Pro (post-MVP), the residency question comes back sharply. See §9.

### 9. Data residency stance

Supabase is in `ap-south-1` (Mumbai). For the MVP as re-scoped (no customer document upload, no named-officials-as-structured-fields), we are storing: public procurement notices + workspace names + user emails on the allowlist. Public info + minimal PII.

This is **not a launch blocker** for the pilot but must not drift. Two threads to keep sharp:

- When Pro adds document upload, we're storing customer confidential financials in Mumbai. That is a real question.
- Bangladesh's data protection regime moved in 2025 and 2026; the read here should be counsel-verified, not treated as settled.

Sharpen the lawyer question before it goes: ask specifically about (a) customer-uploaded corporate documents and (b) personal data of named officials. Do not ask about "procurement data" in the abstract — general questions get general answers.

## Consequences

- Phase 1 plan revised in-place (`proposals/active/phase-1-source-ingestion/plan.md`) with adapter list, PII rule, eExperience-as-lookup, and estimate.
- `/workspaces/new` UI change ships in the same PR as this ADR.
- Phase 2 plan (whenever drafted) inherits BPPA + NOA + APP.
- Phase 3 plan inherits the grade vocabulary + boundaries approach.
- Phase 4 plan inherits the checklist-not-percentage stance.
- Phase 5 + 6 plans inherit the scope-cut list.

## Alternatives considered

- **Keep the original SoT and note deviations only in each phase plan.** Rejected — creates drift; readers would need to hold two documents in their head. This ADR concentrates the delta into one place.
- **Rewrite the SoT.** Rejected — `raw/` is workspace-immutable by convention, and the SoT still describes the vast majority of the build correctly. The changes doc + this ADR are the delta of record.
- **Fold the individual/team scope cuts silently.** Rejected — explicit is safer; the columns and code paths for these features already exist in the schema and should be documented as intentionally-hidden, not deleted.
